3. ASTM E119 Performance: Governing Criterion and Simulation Crossing Times

ASTM E119-24 Section 8 establishes two distinct thermal-transmission performance end-points on the unexposed face of a non-load-bearing wall assembly, each evaluated relative to the initial ambient temperature measured at the start of the test (t = 0 min):

Average Unexposed-Face Temperature Rise: The arithmetic mean of nine thermocouples symmetrically distributed across the unexposed face shall not exhibit a temperature rise exceeding 139 °C (250 °F) above the initial ambient temperature.

Single-Point Unexposed-Face Temperature Rise: The temperature rise recorded at any single thermocouple on the unexposed face shall not exhibit a temperature rise exceeding 181 °C (325 °F) above the initial ambient temperature.

Per ASTM E119 §8 and Section 1 of the External Standards Digest rev 1, the reported fire endurance of the assembly is governed by whichever criterion is reached earlier in time. The reported endurance is not the later of the two, nor is it any mathematical average or interpolation between them.

In the companion simulation (archived in E119_companion_sim_results.csv), the initial ambient temperature at t = 0 min is 21.0 °C, which matches the laboratory ambient of 21.0 °C reported in Red Butte Report RBFMT-25-0918 §3.4. Consequently, the governing temperature thresholds in the simulation are:

Average Rise Threshold: T_avg_threshold = 21.0 °C + 139.0 °C = 160.0 °C.

Single-Point Threshold: T_max_threshold = 21.0 °C + 181.0 °C = 202.0 °C.

3.1 Crossing Times vs Tripping Samples

In compliance with Marguerite Falk's directive and the firm's QA procedure, time end-points are evaluated using continuous linear crossing times rather than discrete sampling intervals that trip the criteria. The discrete time series is logged at 1.0 min intervals in E119_companion_sim_results.csv. Linear interpolation between bounding samples yields the following crossing times:

1. Single-Point Criterion (T_unexposed_max_C reaching 202.0 °C):
   • At time t = 130 min: T_unexposed_max_C = 200.5 °C (rise = 179.5 °C above ambient).
   • At time t = 131 min: T_unexposed_max_C = 202.1 °C (rise = 181.1 °C above ambient).
   • Linear crossing time calculation:
     t_cross = 130 min + [ (202.0 °C - 200.5 °C) / (202.1 °C - 200.5 °C) ] × (131 min - 130 min)
     t_cross = 130 min + [ 1.5 °C / 1.6 °C ] × 1.0 min = 130 min + 0.9375 min = 130.9375 min ≈ 130.94 min.
   • Crossing time to the nearest whole minute: 131 min.
   • Discrete integer sample that trips the threshold: t = 131 min (T = 202.1 °C >= 202.0 °C).

2. Average Unexposed-Face Criterion (T_unexposed_avg_C reaching 160.0 °C):
   • At time t = 176 min: T_unexposed_avg_C = 159.9 °C (rise = 138.9 °C above ambient).
   • At time t = 177 min: T_unexposed_avg_C = 160.9 °C (rise = 139.9 °C above ambient).
   • Linear crossing time calculation:
     t_cross = 176 min + [ (160.0 °C - 159.9 °C) / (160.9 °C - 159.9 °C) ] × (177 min - 176 min)
     t_cross = 176 min + [ 0.1 °C / 1.0 °C ] × 1.0 min = 176 min + 0.1000 min = 176.1000 min ≈ 176.10 min.
   • Crossing time to the nearest whole minute: 176 min.
   • Discrete integer sample that trips the threshold: t = 177 min (T = 160.9 °C >= 160.0 °C).

Governing Determination: The single-point unexposed-face rise criterion is reached at 131 min (crossing time 130.94 min), which occurs 45 min before the average-rise criterion is reached at 176 min (crossing time 176.10 min). Therefore, the single-point criterion GOVERNS the companion simulation endurance. The simulation prediction S = 131 min cited in the Phase 1 Report rev B Section 7 stub STANDS as correct.

5. Model Error Formulation, Acceptance Criterion, and Uncertainty Analysis

In accordance with ASME V&V 20-2009 and Section 3 of CFDME Validation Procedure rev 2, validation is evaluated by comparing the model error E against the firm's quantitative acceptance criterion and against the combined validation uncertainty u_val. Validation is never reported as a binary yes/no statement.

5.1 Model Error Computation

The model error E is defined as the signed difference between the simulation prediction S and the certified experimental data D:
   S = 131 min (companion simulation single-point crossing time, rounded to whole minutes)
   D = 118 min (certified physical test endurance from Red Butte Report RBFMT-25-0918 §5.2)
   E = S - D = 131 min - 118 min = +13 min.
(If evaluated using unrounded crossing times: E = 130.9375 min - 118 min = +12.9375 min ≈ +12.94 min).
The explicit positive sign (+) is retained in all reporting. A positive error of +13 min denotes that the numerical simulation over-predicts fire endurance by 13 min (+11.0% relative to D), meaning the simulation transmits heat through the wall slower than observed in the physical test.

5.2 Firm Acceptance Criterion

Section 5 of CFDME Validation Procedure rev 2 defines the firm's primary acceptance criterion as a scaled tolerance band:
   |E| <= 10.0% of D
For certified endurance D = 118 min, the allowable error bound is:
   0.10 × 118 min = 11.8 min (limit as it falls).
This establishes the acceptable simulation prediction interval as:
   S_acceptable = D ± 10%·D = 118 min ± 11.8 min = [106.2 min, 129.8 min].
Primary Comparison: |E| = 13 min (or 12.94 min unrounded). Because 13 min > 11.8 min (an error of 11.0% vs the 10.0% threshold), the companion simulation FAILS the firm's primary acceptance criterion.

5.3 Validation Uncertainty Build-Up (u_val)

Section 4 of CFDME Validation Procedure rev 2 mandates that validation uncertainty u_val be synthesized as the root-sum-square (RSS) combination of three independent, mutually exclusive uncertainty components established for conjugate thermal ASTM E119 simulations:
   1. Numerical uncertainty, u_num = ±4 min (discretization, time-stepping, and iterative convergence across grid studies).
   2. Input uncertainty, u_inp = ±6 min (propagation of material thermal conductivity, density, specific heat, and cavity radiation/emissivity).
   3. Experimental uncertainty, u_exp = ±5 min (Type-K thermocouple instrumentation, placement, and furnace acquisition uncertainty).
Combining by root-sum-square:
   u_val = sqrt( u_num² + u_inp² + u_exp² ) = sqrt( (4 min)² + (6 min)² + (5 min)² )
   u_val = sqrt( 16 min² + 36 min² + 25 min² ) = sqrt( 77 min² ) = ±8.77496... min ≈ ±8.77 min.
Per Section 4, u_val is reported to whole minutes: u_val = ±9 min for this class of problem.

5.4 Secondary Comparison Against u_val and Error Characterization

Section 5 of the Validation Procedure establishes the secondary diagnostic test: comparing |E| against u_val:
   |E| = 13 min
   u_val = 9 min (or 8.77 min unrounded)
Comparison: |E| = 13 min > u_val = 9 min.
Physical Significance: If |E| were less than or equal to u_val, the discrepancy would sit within the combined noise floor of numerical and experimental uncertainty and would be characterized as measurement scatter. However, because |E| strictly exceeds u_val, the discrepancy is RESOLVED AS A MODEL ERROR. The simulation's +13 min over-prediction is statistically distinct from experimental noise and represents an actionable modeling defect that cannot be explained away by measurement uncertainty.

5.5 Largest Prediction Clearing the Acceptance Criterion

The upper limit of the firm's acceptance band for D = 118 min is:
   S_max = D + 0.10 × D = 118 min + 11.8 min = 129.8 min (limit as it falls).
Any simulation prediction S <= 129.8 min satisfies |E| <= 11.8 min. Expressed as an integer to the nearest whole minute, the LARGEST PREDICTION THAT WOULD HAVE CLEARED THE CRITERION is 129 min. (A prediction of 130 min would produce E = +12 min, which exceeds the 11.8 min limit).
Note on Client Context: It is illuminating that Bianca Rennert's proposed figure of 129 min represents exactly the maximum integer value that would clear the criterion against D = 118 min, or conversely, if D were moved to 129 min, S = 131 min would produce E = +2 min (|E| <= 12.9 min). This underscores why the firm's strict prohibition on unverified client data amendments exists: adopting 129 min would manufacture an artificial validation by manipulating the reference anchor.

6. Key Diagnostic Comparisons and Model Rework Allocation

To diagnose the physical origin of the +13 min model error, two quantitative comparisons required by firm review are examined: (1) the time separation in minutes between the average-rise and single-point criteria in the test versus the run, and (2) the temperature elevation of the governing single point above the nine-point mean at the moment of governance in each.

6.1 Comparison 1: Separation Between Criteria (Average Rise vs Single Point)

In physical testing and numerical simulation of cavity walls with metallic ties, the single-point criterion trips earlier than the average-rise criterion because the ties act as localized thermal bridges. The time separation between the two criteria measures how effectively the model captures the thermal bridging dynamics relative to the bulk concrete leaves:

Physical Test (Red Butte RBFMT-25-0918): Average criterion reached at 163 min; single-point criterion reached at 118 min.
Separation: Delta_t_test = 163 min - 118 min = 45 min.

Companion Simulation (E119 Run): Average criterion crossed at 176.10 min (176 min); single-point criterion crossed at 130.94 min (131 min).
Separation: Delta_t_sim = 176 min - 131 min = 45 min (exact crossing difference: 176.10 min - 130.94 min = 45.16 min ≈ 45 min).

Comparison: Delta_t_test = 45 min vs Delta_t_sim = 45 min (difference: 0 min using rounded values; 0.16 min exact). The time lag between localized tie-driven breakthrough and bulk-wall thermal breakthrough is virtually IDENTICAL in the test and the run.

6.2 Comparison 2: Governing Point Elevation Above Nine-Point Mean at Governance

The second comparison measures how far the local hot spot (governing point) sits above the nine-point spatial average on the unexposed face at the exact instant the single-point criterion is reached:

Physical Test (Red Butte RBFMT-25-0918): At governance (t = 118 min), TC-6 recorded a temperature rise of 181 °C above ambient (absolute temperature: 202.0 °C). The nine-thermocouple average rise at that instant stood at approximately 96 °C above ambient (absolute temperature: 117.0 °C) per Section 5.2 of the Red Butte report.
Elevation above mean: Delta_T_test = 181 °C - 96 °C = 85 °C (or 85 K difference).

Companion Simulation (E119 Run): At governance (t = 131 min), T_unexposed_max_C = 202.1 °C (rise = 181.1 °C above ambient). T_unexposed_avg_C at that instant is 110.9 °C (rise = 89.9 °C above ambient).
Elevation above mean: Delta_T_sim = 202.1 °C - 110.9 °C = 91.2 °C (or 91.2 K difference).
(At exact crossing t = 130.94 min: T_max = 202.0 °C, interpolated T_avg = 110.83 °C, giving Delta_T_sim = 91.17 °C ≈ 91.2 °C).

Comparison: Delta_T_test = 85 °C vs Delta_T_sim = 91.2 °C (difference: 6.2 °C, or 6.2 K). The degree of thermal peaking over the tie course in the simulation matches the physical test within 6.2 °C.

6.3 What These Comparisons Reveal About the Model and Where Rework Belongs

These two diagnostic comparisons yield a profound engineering conclusion regarding the CFD model's internal physics:
1. The local thermal-bridging physics through the steel ties is modeled with excellent physical fidelity. The simulation reproduces the 45 min temporal separation between local tie failure and bulk failure exactly, and captures the local temperature peak within 6.2 °C (91.2 °C vs 85 °C).
2. Crucially, BOTH performance end-points exhibit the identical over-prediction of +13 min:
   • Single-point error: 131 min - 118 min = +13 min.
   • Average-rise error: 176 min - 163 min = +13 min.
Because both the local hot spot and the nine-point average lag the physical test by the exact same 13 minutes, THE MODEL ERROR IS NOT CAUSED BY TIE MODELING, TIE EMBEDMENT, OR LOCAL TIE MESH REFINEMENT. Rather, the entire assembly is undergoing a uniform, global thermal delay.

Allocation of Rework: Engineering rework belongs strictly in the GLOBAL BULK THERMAL PROPERTIES of the printed concrete leaves and in the FURNACE EXPOSED-FACE HEAT TRANSFER BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:
• Bulk Concrete Thermal Inertia: The volumetric heat capacity (rho·c_p) and thermal conductivity k(T) of the printed siliceous-aggregate concrete. If the simulation used constant concrete properties (k = 1.4 W/(m·K), rho = 2200 kg/m³, c_p = 900 J/(kg·K) from Modeling Standard rev 4 Appendix A) or an idealized moisture curve, it failed to capture concrete dehydration endotherms (free water vaporization at 100 °C and chemically bound water release at 100–300 °C). In physical concrete, latent heat absorption creates a temperature plateau, after which dry concrete conductivity drops and thermal transmission accelerates. A model with excessive effective thermal inertia delays overall heating by ~13 min.
• Exposed-Face Boundary Conditions: Radiative and convective heat flux coupling from the furnace gas to the concrete surface. Under-predicting furnace gas emissivity, surface absorptivity, or convective heat transfer coefficients retards heat penetration uniformly across the entire wall.
• Unexposed-Face Boundary Conditions: Over-predicting convective or radiative heat dissipation to the ambient room on the back face.
Rework DOES NOT belong in the wall ties, tie conductivity, tie contact resistance, or local tie mesh resolution.

Current Status of Rework: Has any of this rework been started yet? NO. As of today, 10/10/2025, no rework on the E119 companion simulation or its material/boundary definitions has been initiated. No revised case dictionaries, updated thermophysical property tables, or re-run submissions exist on the firm's file server.

8. Wildfire Claim Analysis: Scope Limitations, Gap Closure, and Future Reporting Form

A central requirement of Section 7 of CFDME Validation Procedure rev 2 and Section 4 of the External Standards Digest rev 1 is enforcing the strict boundary between standard ASTM E119 furnace ratings and real-world wildland-urban interface (WUI) wildfire performance.

8.1 What This Validation Exercise Does and Does Not Establish

What This Exercise Does Establish:
• It evaluates the computational model's ability to simulate 1D/2D conjugate heat transfer through a 305 mm double-wythe printed concrete cavity wall under the standardized building time-temperature curve of ASTM E119.
• It establishes that the model reproduces the 45 min thermal bridging offset caused by metallic ties, but exhibits a global thermal delay of +13 min (+11.0%), failing the firm's 10.0% validation criterion.

What This Exercise DOES NOT Establish:
• It DOES NOT validate or establish Ocotillo's marketing positioning that 'our 3D-printed concrete envelope significantly outperforms conventional wood-frame construction in a wildland-urban-interface wildfire event.'
• It does not predict the survival time, structural integrity, spalling resistance, or habitable conditions of an Ocotillo structure during an actual WUI wildfire.
The physical reasons for this limitation are fundamental:
1. Radical Regime & Heat Flux Discrepancy: ASTM E119 is an enclosed, slowly rising, uniform compartment furnace fire that reaches ~927 °C at 1 hour and ~1010 °C at 2 hours. A Sonoran buffelgrass WUI wildfire is an open-atmosphere, wind-driven, highly turbulent flaming front. It produces rapid, near-instantaneous convective and radiative heat flux spikes (>100 kW/m²), severe wind-tilted flame impingement, intense ember/firebrand showers, and short flame front residence times (typically 1–5 minutes) followed by smoldering ground fuels. Thermal transmission under a 2-hour uniform furnace soak has no direct physical correspondence to transient wildfire front exposure.
2. Complete Absence of Wood-Frame Baseline Data: Outperformance is inherently a comparative claim. Neither Red Butte Report RBFMT-25-0918 nor the companion CFD simulation evaluated, tested, or modeled a conventional wood-frame assembly (e.g., 2×6 wood studs with gypsum board, fiberglass batt insulation, OSB sheathing, and exterior stucco/cladding). In the absence of a verified, direct baseline under identical boundary conditions, any claim of outperformance is scientifically unproven.

8.2 What Would Be Required to Close the Validation Gap

To scientifically close the gap and substantiate a wildfire performance or comparative outperformance claim, Ocotillo and The CFD ME would require:
1. Wildfire-Representative Physical Testing: Execution of large-scale physical testing of the 305 mm printed concrete wall under realistic wildland fire exposures (e.g., radiant panel testing per ASTM E2707, burn-table experiments, or wildfire flame generators delivering the 10,040 kW/m Byram fireline intensity characterized in the Phase 1 scoping memo). This testing must log time-resolved heat fluxes, surface temperatures, cavity temperatures, and unexposed-face responses (such as the Red Butte confirmatory testing window tentatively scheduled for 10/27/2025).
2. Direct Baseline Physical Testing or Validated Modeling: Subjecting an identical-footprint conventional wood-frame wall assembly to the exact same physical fire exposure and numerical simulation protocol.
3. Verification of Firebrand Vulnerability: Evaluating penetrations, coping joints, eaves, and weep holes against ember intrusion, which is the primary ignition mechanism in WUI wildfire disasters.

8.3 Mandatory Reporting Form for Any Future Comparison
